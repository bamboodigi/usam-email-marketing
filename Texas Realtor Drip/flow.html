Texas realtor drip: flow and reasoning

Six cold emails to Texas MLS agents about one product: a second mortgage on a rental the client doesn't live in, for business purposes only. The goal is an introduction to a client who owns a rental, not an application from the agent.

Draft 2026-10-14, revised 2026-10-15 after nine rounds of a five-persona reader panel built from the MLS data (subjects, asks, the terms line now in sends 1, 4 and 6, one send 2 for every agent). Nothing has been sent, loaded or scheduled. Sources: Drip plan.md, AUDIT-strategy-2026-10-14.md, AUDIT-accuracy-compliance-2026-10-14.md and the four PANEL files (the panel was four AI agents playing Texas agents, not real agents).

The flow

Why it's built this way

The audience is an agent, not a borrower. The agent earns nothing from the loan, so every email has to help them close their own next deal or look good to an investor client.

Send 1 · day 0

Start with the agent's sale

An investor client's next down payment may already be sitting in a rental they own. They can borrow against it and keep their first mortgage. The email says who USA Mortgage is and who qualifies: a 1-4 unit rental worth $100K or more, held personally or in an LLC, short-term rentals included.

Reply "rental"

The MLS list has no "has investor clients" flag, so the one-word reply is the filter (plan section 3). The strategy audit's top finding: nothing told a stranger who USA Mortgage is.

Send 2 · day 6

Timing is the reason to call early

The second mortgage typically closes in 3-4 weeks, subject to underwriting. Starting it before the offer keeps the agent's closing from waiting on it.

Next time a buyer mentions a rental they own, cc Jack

Moved up from old E5. The Austin panelist said 3-4 weeks is slow against a 25-30 day close, which is exactly why this email says to start first.

Send 3 · day 13

Answer the client's first question

"Do I have to refinance?" A cash-out refi replaces the first mortgage. A second leaves it in place. The comparison table is cut to 3 rows.

Forward the comparison guide

Forwarding a guide is a low-risk ask. The team-lead panelist wanted one email clean enough to forward.

Send 4 · day 20

Show what happens after an intro

What the client is asked (value, first mortgage balance, rent, what the money is for), what's checked, the 3-4 week typical close, lump sum or line of credit.

With the client's OK, cc Jack

New email. The strategy audit rated "no process shown" HIGH. All four panelists objected to sending a client's numbers, so the client sends their own after the intro.

Send 5 · day 34

Three plain checks, plus Texas

A rental they don't live in, rent that covers the payments, room under 80% of value across all loans. Texas homestead rules in two sentences, then the market's counties and local page.

Know a seller or past client with a rental? With their OK, cc Jack

Merges old E3 and E4. The Houston panelist stopped reading at the constitutional-law section, so the legal citations are gone. Before this rebuild, 8,673 Houston and San Antonio agents had no local version.

Send 6 · day 48

A real exit

One client in mind? The terms in one line (fixed rates from 6.99%, typical terms, subject to underwriting and market conditions); since 2026-10-15 the same terms also appear in sends 1 and 4. It says plainly that it's the last email.

Reply with an intro, or do nothing

The strategy audit flagged that the old exit moved people onto a monthly list. Exit now means exit.

Rules every email follows

  • Hook first in the preview text. The old preview text opened with the disclosure, which the Houston panelist named as the single biggest problem. The "Advertisement" line is now the first line of the body.
  • Never asks the agent for a client's numbers, and never says USA Mortgage will tell them whether a client is worth applying (the accuracy audit's Reg B finding).
  • Facts only from the approved program data: $50K to $1M, up to 80% CLTV, 660 credit score, 1.00 DSCR, 3-4 week typical close.
  • No referral-fee line, per Blake.
  • The same layout as the rest of the library: header with Apply now, calculator banner, Keep reading cards, CTA band, signatures and footer, per Blake.
  • Business purpose only in every email, and the legal line in every footer.

Opens aren't used: Apple Mail inflates them. There's no baseline for cold agent lists, so the first send sets it (plan section 4).
